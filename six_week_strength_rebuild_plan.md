# Six-Week Strength Rebuild Plan

**Purpose:** Rebuild strength over six weeks and work towards reclaiming previous one-rep maxes, while managing fatigue and avoiding interference between sessions.

## Previous best one-rep maxes

| Lift | Previous 1RM |
|---|---:|
| Bench press | 100 kg |
| Squat | 170 kg |
| Deadlift | 200 kg |

## Recent training baseline

Based on recent Hevy logs:

- **Bench press (4 November 2026):** 80 kg × 5, then 90 kg × 3 at RPE 9, followed by 72.5 kg × 6 × 2.
- **Squat (6 November 2026):** 132.5 kg × 5 × 3 sets.
- **Deadlift (3 November 2026):** 150 kg × 4 × 3 sets.

These are recent training performances, not confirmed current one-rep maxes.

## Rolling training sequence

Follow the next session in order rather than tying workouts to fixed weekdays. If a session is missed, continue with the next one; do not double up to catch up.

1. **Upper A — Heavy bench**
2. **Lower A — Squat**
3. **Run 1 — Easy aerobic**
4. **Upper B — Bench volume**
5. **Lower B — Deadlift and back**
6. **Run 2 — Longer easy run**
7. **Full rest day**

Preserve exactly one full rest day in every seven-day period. Keep both runs conversational rather than turning them into hard conditioning sessions. Shorten or skip a run if it interferes with lifting recovery; do not make up missed running later in the week.

## Running progression

The purpose of the runs is to build aerobic fitness while keeping fatigue low enough to support the strength work. For the first six weeks, use two easy runs per rolling seven-day cycle:

- **Run 1 — Easy aerobic:** shorter, relaxed run at a conversational effort (about 3–4/10). You should be able to speak in full sentences.
- **Run 2 — Longer easy:** slightly longer run at the same conversational effort. Do not chase pace or turn it into a tempo run.
- Keep the effort easy throughout. If breathing becomes laboured or you cannot speak in full sentences, slow down or use short walk breaks.
- Increase duration only when the previous week felt comfortable and lifting performance and recovery remained stable. The times below are ceilings/targets, not mandatory minimums.

| Week | Run 1 — Easy aerobic | Run 2 — Longer easy |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | 25 min | 35 min |
| 2 | 30 min | 40 min |
| 3 | 30 min | 45 min |
| 4 | 35 min | 45 min |
| 5 — Deload | 20–25 min | 30–35 min |
| 6 — Testing | 15–20 min easy, if recovered | Skip or 20 min very easy |

### Running placement and adjustment rules

- Keep the rolling order: Upper A → Lower A → Run 1 → Upper B → Lower B → Run 2 → full rest.
- If legs feel heavy after squats or deadlifts, reduce the next run by 10–15 minutes or skip it.
- Do not add intervals, hills, or hard tempo work during this six-week strength rebuild.
- If a run affects the quality of the next lifting session, reduce running duration before reducing the main-lift work.
- During week 6, prioritise the three lift tests. Running is optional and should be skipped if there is any meaningful residual fatigue.
- Signs the running dose is appropriate: easy pace gradually improves at the same conversational effort, runs finish with plenty left in reserve, and squat/deadlift performance remains stable.

After the six-week block, if strength recovery is consistently good, consider adding a small amount of faster running once per week while keeping the other run easy. Do not add intensity and duration at the same time.

## Week 6 — Testing

The following are conditional targets, not guaranteed attempts. Only increase to the next attempt if the previous one is technically sound and moves well. Do not force a maximal grind.

### Bench press

Previous best: **100 kg**

- First attempt: 90 kg
- Second attempt: 95 kg
- Final attempt: 97.5–100 kg, depending on the second attempt

Goal: reclaim 100 kg.

### Squat

Previous best: **170 kg**

- First attempt: 150 kg
- Second attempt: 160 kg
- Final attempt: 165–170 kg, depending on the second attempt

Goal: reclaim 170 kg.

### Deadlift

Previous best: **200 kg**

- First attempt: 175 kg
- Second attempt: 185 kg
- Final attempt: 190–200 kg, depending on the second attempt

Goal: move towards 200 kg.

If an attempt is already a grind or technique breaks down, stop there. A clean sub-maximal result is better than a failed PR attempt. Use a competent spotter and safety bars/rack safeties for bench and squat. If not ready for a true 1RM, use a heavy clean triple to estimate strength instead.

### Suggested testing-week layout

| Day | Session |
|---|---|
| Monday | Bench test |
| Tuesday | Easy recovery |
| Wednesday | Squat test |
| Thursday | Easy recovery |
| Friday | Deadlift test |
| Saturday | Easy run or rest |
| Sunday | Full rest |

Allow at least 48 hours between tests. Skip running if it compromises recovery.

## Progress and recovery rules

1. Do not force progression. If you cannot complete the prescribed sets with about 1–3 reps in reserve, repeat the load or reduce it.
2. Avoid failed reps during the five-week build.
3. Do not double up sessions after missed days.
4. If your lower back is still fatigued from squats, do not force the deadlift session.
5. Keep runs conversational and reduce their duration or skip them when needed.
6. Record weight, reps and RPE for every main lift.
7. Prioritise good technique and repeatable training over hitting every planned number.

## Performance checkpoints

By the end of week 4, monitor whether:

- Bench: 80 kg is controlled for 4 × 4.
- Squat: 140 kg is controlled for 3 × 4.
- Deadlift: 160 kg is controlled for 3 × 3.

If these are unusually difficult, adjust week 5 and testing attempts down. If they are controlled with reps in reserve, proceed cautiously with the planned taper and tests.

## Important context for future reviews

- This is a proposed six-week plan based on the recent logs and previous bests listed above; it has not been confirmed as completed.
- The goal is to get close to previous maxes, with PRs as a bonus—not to guarantee all three in six weeks.
- Bench 100 kg is a reasonable target to work towards. Squat 170 kg is ambitious but plausible; deadlift 200 kg is the largest uncertainty based on the recent training data.
- When reviewing this plan later, compare actual completed sets, reps, RPE, recovery, and any missed or modified sessions against the planned progression. Adjust testing attempts based on actual week-five performance.

## Running progression (two runs per rolling week)

The runs support aerobic fitness without compromising the four strength sessions. Keep both runs conversational and easy (about 3–4/10 effort); you should be able to speak in full sentences. Treat durations as ceilings, not obligations.

| Week | Run 1 — easy aerobic | Run 2 — longer easy |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | 25 min | 35 min |
| 2 | 30 min | 40 min |
| 3 | 30 min | 45 min |
| 4 | 35 min | 45 min |
| 5 — deload | 20–25 min | 30–35 min |
| 6 — testing | 15–20 min easy, if recovered | Skip or 20 min very easy |

### Placement in the rolling schedule

1. Upper A — heavy bench
2. Lower A — squat
3. Run 1 — easy aerobic
4. Upper B — bench volume
5. Lower B — deadlift and back
6. Run 2 — longer easy
7. Full rest day

Keep exactly one full rest day in each seven-day cycle. Do not double up missed sessions or runs. If your legs feel heavy after squats or deadlifts, shorten the next run by 10–15 minutes or skip it; do not make it up later.

### Running progression and recovery rules

- Increase duration only when the previous week's running felt comfortable and lifting performance remained solid.
- Do not chase pace during this six-week strength rebuild. Let pace improve naturally at the same easy effort.
- Finish each run feeling that you could have continued.
- If persistent leg fatigue appears or squat/deadlift performance drops, hold the duration steady or reduce it.
- In week 6, prioritize recovery and testing; omit the longer run if it could interfere with the tests.
- After the six-week block, if recovery is good, consider adding one small controlled faster-running session per week while keeping the other run easy.
